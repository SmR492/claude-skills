# Chesterton's fence

Ref: `BL:chestertons-fence` · Category: principle · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 71. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-chestertons-fence.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

A principle of cautionary conservatism stating that before changing or removing something, one should first understand why it exists. Even if a practice or object seems pointless, it likely served some purpose we may not be aware of. It is named after writer and philosopher G.K. Chesterton, who described it in his 1929 book 'The Thing: Why I Am a Catholic' using the metaphor of a fence in a field that should be investigated before being torn down. It is invoked in fields such as engineering, law and public policy to avoid unintended consequences.

- Understand why something exists before changing or removing it
- Metaphor of a fence whose purpose should be investigated before tearing it down
- Named after G.K. Chesterton (1929 book 'The Thing: Why I Am a Catholic')
- Applied in engineering, law and public policy
- Encourages critical thinking to avoid unintended consequences
