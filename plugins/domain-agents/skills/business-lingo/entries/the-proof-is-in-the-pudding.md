# The proof is in the pudding

Ref: `BL:the-proof-is-in-the-pudding` · Category: term · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 52. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-the-proof-is-in-the-pudding.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

"The proof is in the pudding" means the effectiveness or quality of something can only be determined by putting it to the test or trying it out, emphasizing practical experience and experimentation over theory. It is believed to have originated in England in the 1600s, with the original version being "the proof of the pudding is in the eating." It is used in business, politics, and other contexts to emphasize the importance of results.
