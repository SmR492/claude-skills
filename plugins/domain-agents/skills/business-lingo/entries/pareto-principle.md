# The Pareto Principle (The 80/20 Rule)

Ref: `BL:pareto-principle` · Category: principle · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 69. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-pareto-principle.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

Named after Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto, this principle suggests that roughly 80% of the effects come from 20% of the causes. It is applied across economics, business, management and personal productivity - for example describing income/wealth distribution or customer profitability. In management and productivity it is used to identify and prioritize the ~20% of activities that produce the greatest impact. The 80/20 split is not a hard-and-fast rule and actual percentages may vary by context.

- Roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes
- Economics: small percentage of population holds large percentage of wealth
- Business: small percentage of customers accounts for large percentage of revenue
- Management/productivity: focus on the 20% of activities with the greatest impact
- Actual percentages may vary depending on context
