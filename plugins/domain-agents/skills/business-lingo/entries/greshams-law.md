# Gresham's Law

Gresham's Law is an economic principle stating "bad money drives out good." When two types of currency circulate, people spend the lower-quality (debased) currency while hoarding the higher-quality (full-bodied) currency, so the higher-quality currency is removed from circulation. It was named after Sir Thomas Gresham, a 16th-century English financier who observed debasement under King Henry VIII. It has since been applied to phenomena such as fake news driving out credible journalism and inferior products pushing out high-quality ones.
