# Metcalfe's Law

Ref: `BL:metcalfes-law` · Category: law · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 63. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-metcalfes-law.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

Metcalfe's Law states that the value of a telecommunications network is proportional to the square of the number of connected users in the system. It was first proposed by Robert Metcalfe, co-inventor of Ethernet, and applies to all networks that allow communication between users. As users join, the number of possible connections grows quadratically (roughly the square of the number of users), making the network more valuable; it is often used to explain the success of social networks like Facebook and LinkedIn. A key criticism is that it assumes all connections are of equal value.
