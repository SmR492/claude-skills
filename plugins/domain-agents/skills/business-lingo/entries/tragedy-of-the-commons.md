# The Tragedy of the Commons

Ref: `BL:tragedy-of-the-commons` · Category: term · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 72. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-tragedy-of-the-commons.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

An economic theory describing a situation where a shared resource is overused or exploited due to a lack of ownership and control. First introduced by British economist William Forster Lloyd in the 1830s and later popularized by American biologist Garrett Hardin in a 1968 paper. When individuals have free, unrestricted access to a shared resource (land, water, air), they tend to overuse it because they prioritize short-term self-interest over the long-term health of the resource. It can be mitigated through ownership rights, regulations (e.g. fishing quotas, grazing limits) or privatization.

- Shared resource overused due to lack of ownership and control
- Introduced by William Forster Lloyd (1830s), popularized by Garrett Hardin (1968)
- Individuals prioritize short-term gains over long-term resource health
- Examples: overfishing, overgrazing common land
- Mitigated via ownership rights, regulations/quotas, or privatization
