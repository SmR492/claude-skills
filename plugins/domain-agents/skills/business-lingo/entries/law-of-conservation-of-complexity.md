# The Law of Conservation of Complexity (Tesler's Law)

Ref: `BL:law-of-conservation-of-complexity` · Category: law · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 67. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-law-of-conservation-of-complexity.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

A design principle formulated by Larry Tesler, a computer scientist who worked for Xerox PARC and Apple. It states that complexity is a finite resource that must be conserved: every increase in complexity in one part of a system must be offset by a corresponding decrease elsewhere. It is a call for simplicity in design, urging designers and developers to minimize unnecessary complexity and focus on the most important features. It is especially relevant to user experience (UX) design, where the goal is intuitive, efficient, satisfying interfaces.

- Complexity is a finite resource; increases in one part must be offset by decreases elsewhere
- Strive to make products as simple and easy to use as possible
- Simplify interfaces by removing unnecessary buttons, menus or elements
- Streamline workflows and reduce the number of steps to complete a task
- Improves accessibility for a wider range of users, including those with disabilities or special needs
