# Red tape

Ref: `BL:red-tape` · Category: term · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 45. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-red-tape.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

The idiom "red tape" refers to excessive bureaucracy, rules, regulations, or administrative procedures that can delay or hinder the progress of a task, project, or decision-making process. It implies unnecessary and often frustrating obstacles that slow down or complicate matters. The origin traces to the 16th century, when official documents in the British government were tied with red tape to indicate their importance or urgency.

- Government and Administration: bureaucratic processes and excessive regulations slow decision-making
- Corporate or Organizational Settings: cumbersome internal procedures, rigid protocols, excessive paperwork
- Legal and Regulatory Matters: complex, lengthy compliance and filing procedures
- Public Services: bureaucratic hurdles affecting healthcare, education, transportation
