# Hyrum's Law

Ref: `BL:hyrums-law` · Category: law · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 62. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-hyrums-law.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

Hyrum's Law refers to the inevitability of compatibility issues when software components depend on one another. It states: "With a sufficient number of users of an API, it does not matter what you promise in the contract: all observable behaviors of your system will be depended on by somebody." It is named after Hyrum Wright, a Google software engineer who articulated the observation around 2011-2012; the term was coined by his colleague Titus Winters and popularized in the book "Software Engineering at Google." Implications: developers should be cautious making changes and should document all observable behaviors, even unintended ones.
