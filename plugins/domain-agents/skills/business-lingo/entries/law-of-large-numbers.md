# The Law of Large Numbers

Ref: `BL:law-of-large-numbers` · Category: law · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 68. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-law-of-large-numbers.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

A fundamental concept in probability theory and statistics stating that as the sample size of a statistical population increases, the sample mean converges to the population mean, becoming a more accurate estimate. It is based on the idea that random events tend to even out over the long run (e.g. coin tosses approach a 50/50 split with more trials). It does not guarantee the sample mean equals the population mean exactly; sampling error always exists but shrinks as sample size grows.

- Larger sample size makes the sample mean a more accurate estimate of the true population mean
- Random events even out over the long run
- Used in insurance to predict future events from past data
- Applied in finance, economics and engineering to estimate probabilities and test hypotheses
- Sampling error remains but decreases with larger samples
