# Sunk cost fallacy

Ref: `BL:sunk-cost-fallacy` · Category: term · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 73. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-sunk-cost-fallacy.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

The sunk cost fallacy occurs when individuals or organizations continue to invest time, resources or effort into a project simply because they have already invested a significant amount. Sunk costs are costs already incurred that cannot be recovered regardless of future action. The concept holds that rational decisions should be based on future prospects rather than past investments, so decision-making should ignore sunk costs and instead weigh prospective costs, benefits and opportunity costs.

- Past decisions: sunk costs result from past decisions or actions (e.g. money on a failed project)
- Irretrievable expenses: costs already paid that cannot be recovered
- Decision-making: avoid considering sunk costs; base decisions on future costs and benefits
- Opportunity costs: consider benefits foregone by choosing one alternative over another
