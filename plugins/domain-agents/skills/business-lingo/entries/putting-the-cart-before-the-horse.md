# Putting the cart before the horse

Ref: `BL:putting-the-cart-before-the-horse` · Category: term · Entry version: 1
Source: business-lingo-guide Seite 43. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/BL-putting-the-cart-before-the-horse.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

The idiom "putting the cart before the horse" means doing things in the wrong order or prioritizing things incorrectly, reversing the natural or logical sequence of events. It originates from a horse pulling a cart, where placing the cart in front of the horse would be inefficient and illogical. Metaphorically it cautions against acting out of sequence — for example, focusing on specific tasks before establishing clear goals, objectives, or a strategic plan — and reminds one to follow a logical order with proper planning and foresight.
