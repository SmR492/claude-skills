# Usability friction

Source: ui-ux-design-guide Seite 78. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/UX-usability-friction.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

Usability friction refers to the resistance or obstacles users may encounter when interacting with a product, encompassing any elements that make the experience less smooth or efficient. Reducing friction is a key goal in product design because it can improve user satisfaction, increase engagement, and improve overall product performance. The text lists common friction areas.

- UI: complicated or cluttered interfaces
- Slowness: slow loading/running times
- Onboarding: lengthy or complex sign-up or setup
- Navigation: unclear navigation or confusing menus
- Data Entry: long or complex forms
- Errors: inadequate error messages or lack of feedback
- Payments: purchasing processes with too many steps or security complexity
- Inaccessibility: inaccessible design for people with disabilities
- Mobile: inconsistent or poorly responsive design
- Waste: unnecessary steps; Overload: too much information or too many choices
