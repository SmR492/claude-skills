A low-fidelity prototype (also lo-fi or paper prototype) is a simple, rough draft of a product created at the early design stages to quickly and inexpensively test and iterate on ideas. Created with low-cost materials such as paper, cardboard, sticky notes, or wireframes, it provides a basic representation of structure, features, and user flow to gather feedback. It is quick and inexpensive, easily modified, and an important part of the design thinking process.

- Made with low-cost materials: paper, cardboard, sticky notes, or wireframes
- Quick and inexpensive to produce; easily modified and updated as feedback is received
- Examples: sketches on paper/whiteboard, hand-drawn wireframes or flowcharts, cardboard cutouts, low-resolution digital mockups (Balsamiq, Sketch)
- Helps visualize the product and identify usability issues, clarify requirements, generate ideas
