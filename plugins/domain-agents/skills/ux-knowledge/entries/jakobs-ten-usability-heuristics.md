# Jakob's Ten Usability Heuristics

Jakob's Ten Usability Heuristics are ten general principles for interaction design. They cover system status visibility, matching the real world, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, flexibility, minimalist design, error recovery, and help/documentation.

- Visibility of System Status: keep users informed through timely feedback
- Match between System and the Real World: use familiar words, phrases, and concepts, not jargon
- User Control and Freedom: provide a clearly marked 'emergency exit'
- Consistency and Standards: follow conventions so users don't wonder about meaning
- Error Prevention: prevent problems from occurring in the first place
- Recognition Rather Than Recall: make elements, actions, and options visible
- Flexibility and Efficiency of Use: shortcuts for expert users, hidden from novices
- Aesthetic and Minimalist Design: avoid irrelevant information
- Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors: plain-language messages, indicate problem, suggest solution
- Help and Documentation: ideally not needed, but provide documentation to help complete tasks
