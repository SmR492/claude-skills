# Big design up front (BDUF)

Source: ui-ux-design-guide Seite 94. Provenance: bulk extraction 2026-07-02, migrated to Orbit 2026-09-02 (riedl/nsai/doktrin/UX-big-design-up-front-bduf.json); content unverified against a primary source. Treat figures and legal statements as unverified until checked.

Big design up front (BDUF) is a software development approach where developers work on detailed requirements, design documents, and specifications for the entire project before any coding begins, contrasting with agile/iterative methodologies. It is often used in large-scale projects with many stakeholders and dependencies. Proponents argue it provides a clear roadmap, minimizes later changes, and increases the probability of success; criticisms are that it can be time-consuming and costly, risks building a design that does not meet needs, and makes it difficult to change or pivot when new information emerges.

- Contrasts with agile/iterative methodologies
- Pros: clear roadmap, minimizes later changes, higher probability of success
- Cons: time-consuming, costly, hard to pivot; not a one-size-fits-all solution
- Suitability depends on scope, budget, timeline, and stakeholder requirements
