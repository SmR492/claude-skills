# Gibson's affordance theory

Gibson's affordance theory, developed by psychologist James J. Gibson, is a framework explaining how humans perceive and interact with their environment, where affordances are the perceived possibilities for action the environment offers to an individual. It has had significant impact on psychology, cognitive science, human-computer interaction, UI/UX, and design.

- Perception-Action Coupling: perception and action are closely coupled; perception is an active process of perceiving action opportunities
- Direct Perception: perceptual information is rich enough to directly perceive affordances without complex cognitive processes or internal representations
- Environmental Properties: affordances are determined by environmental properties and can be physical and social (e.g. a chair affords sitting, a staircase affords climbing)
- Situated Action: affordances are context-dependent, shaped by goals, intentions, and cultural norms within a specific environment
