# Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is a metric that helps companies evaluate the total cost incurred to acquire a single new customer, including all marketing, advertising, and sales expenses. It is calculated by dividing the total cost of sales and marketing by the number of new customers gained in a time period. A low CAC indicates effective acquisition, while a high CAC may indicate inefficiencies. Comparing CAC to LTV reveals the profitability of acquiring new customers.

- CAC = total sales & marketing cost / new customers gained in a period
- Example: $100,000 spent, 1,000 new customers → CAC of $100
- Low CAC = effective acquisition; high CAC = inefficiency
- If LTV > CAC the company profits on acquisition; if CAC > LTV it loses money
